You're absolutely right to draw this parallel. In digital engineering:

  • NAND is functionally complete and implements Sum of Products (SOP)
  • NOR is functionally complete and implements Product of Sums (POS)

This raises the natural question: if eml(x,y) is the NAND analogue, does a dual NOR analogue exist? Yes — and it's called lme(x,y).

4. The Two Dual Functions

The NAND Analogue — eml(x, y)

$$\boxed{\text{eml}(x,\, y) = e^x - \ln(y)}$$

  • $e^x$ operates in the multiplicative world (lifts $x$ out of additive space)
  • $\ln(y)$ operates in the additive world (pulls $y$ down from multiplicative space)
  • Subtraction is the analogue of negated conjunction (NAND's $\neg\wedge$)
  • Domain: $x \in \mathbb{R},\quad y \in \mathbb{R}^+$
  • Canonical form analogue: Sum of Products (SOP)

The NOR Analogue — lme(x, y)

Applying De Morgan duality: swap $e^x \leftrightarrow \ln(x)$ and swap the roles of the arguments:

$$\boxed{\text{lme}(x,\, y) = \ln(x) - e^y}$$

  • $\ln(x)$ operates in the additive world
  • $e^y$ operates in the multiplicative world
  • Domain: $x \in \mathbb{R}^+,\quad y \in \mathbb{R}$
  • Canonical form analogue: Product of Sums (POS)

6. The Zero Loci — Analogue of Truth Tables

NAND outputs $0$ only when both inputs are $1$. NOR outputs $0$ only when both inputs are $0$.

eml zero locus: $$\text{eml}(x,y) = 0 \iff e^x = \ln y \iff y = e^{e^x}$$ A doubly-exponential curve — an extraordinarily steep boundary in the $(x,y)$ plane.

lme zero locus: $$\text{lme}(x,y) = 0 \iff \ln x = e^y \iff x = e^{e^y}$$

The lme zero locus is the reflection of the eml zero locus across the line $y = x$ — precisely as expected from a dual pair. This mirrors the Boolean symmetry exactly.

7. The Duality Theorem

$$\boxed{\text{lme}(x,\, y) = -\,\text{eml}\!\left(y,\, \tfrac{1}{x}\right)}$$

Proof: $$-\,\text{eml}\!\left(y,\, \frac{1}{x}\right) = -\!\left(e^y - \ln\frac{1}{x}\right) = -e^y + \ln\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^{-1} = \ln x - e^y = \text{lme}(x,\, y) \qquad \blacksquare$$

This is the continuous analogue of De Morgan's theorem — lme is eml with arguments transformed and sign flipped, exactly as NOR is NAND with inputs complemented.

8. Functional Completeness

In Boolean logic, NAND alone suffices to build any circuit. The continuous analogue holds:

From eml alone: $$e^x = \text{eml}(x,\, 1) \qquad \text{since } \ln(1) = 0$$ $$\ln y = -\,\text{eml}(0,\, y) + 1 \qquad \text{since } e^0 = 1$$

From lme alone: $$\ln x = \text{lme}(x,\, 0) \qquad \text{since } e^0 = 1$$ $$e^y = -\,\text{lme}(1,\, y) \qquad \text{since } \ln(1) = 0$$

Both primitive operations ($e^x$ and $\ln$) are recoverable from either eml or lme alone — confirming functional completeness for each.
